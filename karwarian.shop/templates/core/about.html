About Karwar

Discover the pearl of coastal Karnataka

ಕಾರವಾರ — God's Own Coast

Karwar is a beautiful coastal city and the district headquarters of Uttara Kannada in Karnataka, India. Nestled between the majestic Arabian Sea on one side and the lush Western Ghats on the other, Karwar is a paradise for nature lovers and adventure seekers alike.

The city is renowned for its pristine, uncrowded beaches, the serene Kali River, and its rich naval heritage. The Indian Navy's INS Kadamba — the largest naval base on India's western coast — is located here, adding to the city's strategic importance.

Geography & Natural Beauty

Karwar sits at the confluence of the Kali River and the Arabian Sea, surrounded by verdant hills and dense forests. The Sadashivgad Hill Fort offers panoramic views of the sea, river, and the town below. The region is home to diverse marine life and is part of the Western Ghats biodiversity hotspot.

With over 12 beaches, including the famous Karwar Beach, Devbagh Beach, and the secluded Tilmati Beach, the coastline stretches for kilometers of unspoiled beauty. The nearby islands — Oyster Rocks and Devbagh — offer snorkeling, water sports, and unique wildlife encounters.

Culture & Heritage

Karwar has a rich cultural tapestry woven with Konkani, Kannada, and Marathi traditions. The city celebrates vibrant festivals like Karavali Utsav, showcasing local art, music, dance, and cuisine. The famous Durga Temple and Sadashivgad Fort are important landmarks that attract visitors from across the country.

The renowned Indian poet Rabindranath Tagore was so captivated by Karwar's beauty that he wrote about it in his memoirs, calling it one of the most beautiful places he had ever visited.
